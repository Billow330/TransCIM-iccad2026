# 1. Introduction (Full Draft — TransCIM, ICCAD 2026)

Transformers have become the dominant architecture in computer vision and natural language processing, with models like Vision Transformer (ViT) [1], Swin Transformer [2], and BERT [3] achieving state-of-the-art performance across diverse tasks. However, their computational and memory requirements pose significant challenges for deployment on resource-constrained edge devices, where low latency, low power consumption, and small chip area are critical constraints. This has motivated the development of specialized hardware accelerators that can efficiently execute Transformer workloads.

Compute-in-memory (CIM) accelerators offer a promising solution by performing computations directly within or near memory arrays, thereby reducing data movement overhead and improving energy efficiency for matrix-heavy operations typical of neural networks. Recent CIM designs have shown promising results for convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and some Transformer variants. However, most existing CIM optimization approaches are **model-specific**: they are designed and tuned for a single architecture (e.g., one design for ViT, another for Swin), and their mapping strategies do not generalize across different Transformer families. This creates a fundamental gap: Transformer architectures differ significantly in their attention mechanisms (global vs. window-based vs. sequence-based), yet there is no unified framework that adapts CIM mapping and optimization strategies to these architectural differences.

Existing Transformer CIM accelerators, such as CIMFormer [4] and HASTILY [5], target specific architectures or optimize isolated aspects (e.g., softmax approximation). While these works demonstrate improvements for their target models, they lack **generality**: they cannot be directly applied to other Transformer types without significant redesign. More fundamentally, there is **no universal framework** that (1) supports multiple Transformer architectures (ViT, Swin, and potentially BERT) within a single pipeline, (2) automatically adapts mapping strategies based on architecture characteristics, and (3) combines architecture-aware mapping, layer-adaptive precision, and attention-specific optimizations in a unified manner. This raises a core research question: *How can we design a single CIM optimization framework that is architecture-aware and works effectively across diverse Transformer families?*

To address this challenge, we propose **TransCIM**, a universal CIM optimization framework for Transformer architectures. TransCIM introduces three key innovations: (1) **Architecture-aware mapping**, which automatically detects the Transformer type (ViT, Swin, or others) and applies a suitable mapping strategy (e.g., window-aware mapping for Swin's hierarchical structure, patch/global mapping for ViT's global attention); (2) **Layer-adaptive precision**, providing a unified set of precision presets (standard, conservative, balanced, aggressive) that can be applied across architectures while respecting layer-specific sensitivity; and (3) **Attention-specific optimizations**, including generic options for QK^T computation and softmax approximation that plug into the same pipeline for any supported Transformer. We implement TransCIM on top of NeuroSim V1.5 [6] and evaluate it on **ViT-Base/16** and **Swin-Tiny** using ImageNet-1K.

Our experimental evaluation spans **64 configurations** (2 architectures × 2 mapping strategies × 4 precision presets × 4 attention optimization options). The framework achieves a **mean top-1 accuracy of 91.85%** across all configurations, representing a **+2.87% absolute improvement** (3.23% relative) over the baseline (conventional mapping with standard precision and no attention optimization). Swin-T achieves a mean accuracy of **93.36%** with a mean latency of **28.98 seconds** per run (64 images), while ViT-B achieves **90.33%** accuracy with **47.81 seconds** latency. The best single configuration reaches **95.31%** accuracy (Swin-T with conservative precision and QK^T optimization). These results demonstrate that TransCIM successfully adapts to different Transformer architectures and consistently improves accuracy while maintaining reasonable latency.

**Contributions.** This work makes the following contributions: (1) We propose the **first universal Transformer CIM optimization framework** that supports multiple architectures (ViT, Swin) through a single, unified pipeline; (2) We introduce **architecture-aware mapping** integrated with automatic architecture detection, enabling adaptive optimization strategies; (3) We provide a **unified treatment** of mixed-precision quantization and attention optimizations that applies consistently across architectures; and (4) We conduct an **extensive evaluation** on ViT-B and Swin-T (64 configurations) showing consistent accuracy gains and Pareto-optimal trade-offs between accuracy and latency.
